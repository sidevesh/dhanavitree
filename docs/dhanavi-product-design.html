04 / CONVERSATIONAL ONBOARDING

Never say,
“fill in these 24 fields.”

Ask the next useful question. Accept what the owner
already has. Make uncertain information easy to fix.

01

Start with their words

“What do you do, and what do people usually come to you for?”

Business name, category, language and the owner's goals emerge naturally. Ask one thing at a time; allow “skip for now.”

02

Accept the real material

“Have a menu, catalogue, price list or a few photos? You can send them here.”

Text, images and PDFs first. Show uploading → extracting → review. Voice and more document types follow when supported.

03

Place the business

“Where can customers find you? A Maps link or an address works.”

Confirm the pin, address, service area and timezone. A Maps link is a reference—not permission or a guarantee of imported place data.

04

Make the basics answerable

“What are your usual hours, and how should customers contact you?”

Capture public contact, offerings, price qualifiers, delivery/booking rules and holiday uncertainty. Owner decides what is public.

05

Reflect, then activate

“Here's what I'll tell customers. Anything you'd like to change?”

Review a compact business card. Test a sample answer. Publish the customer link only after essentials are confirmed.

MINIMUM TO SHARE THE LINK

Enough knowledge to be useful.

  • Confirmed business identity and public location/service model.
  • At least one offering or a clear description of what the business does.
  • Hours/contact, or explicit “not provided” handling.
  • Owner-approved public profile and a working escalation route.

Unknown delivery charges shouldn't block launch. They should block an invented delivery quote.

UPLOADS ARE A CONVERSATION, TOO

“I found this. Is it right?”

  • Show thumbnails, file names, progress, retry and delete.
  • Keep extracted values in draft until confirmed.
  • Flag unclear digits, currencies, units, and conflicting prices.
  • For unsupported or unreadable material, ask for another file or a typed answer.

“Whatever format” should feel flexible; the interface must still state the formats and size limits actually supported.

✳

Capture once. Refine forever.

Onboarding becomes the ongoing owner conversation: “We're closed on Monday,” “Here's the new menu,” “What should I try this week?” Every update uses the same review-and-save pattern.

07 / UI & UX PATTERNS

Familiar enough to use.
Distinct enough to trust.

Borrow messaging conventions from WhatsApp.
Give business decisions their own clear structure.
Don't recreate an inbox where a decision is needed.

01

A home with a hierarchy

Waiting customers first, time-sensitive business details second, optional ideas third. Empty state: “You're all caught up,” with a useful next action.

02

One primary action per card

Answer, confirm, or explore. Show the effect before commitment. Keep edit and dismiss available without competing visually.

03

Progress without pressure

“Your link is nearly ready” with a few clear essentials. Let the conversation resume. Avoid fake precision in an adaptive onboarding percentage.

04

Source chips over certainty theatre

“Owner confirmed,” “Needs review,” “Expired.” Avoid fabricated model confidence percentages or implying every AI answer is verified.

05

Messages plus structured outcomes

Keep the natural conversation, but show durable cards for offerings, hours, saved facts, tasks and creative drafts. Make the saved result findable later.

06

Plain language, local voice

Remember preferred language and units. Confirm ambiguous dates and prices. Support mixed-language conversation without making assumptions about location or religion.

07

Mobile owner, mobile customer

Owner navigation becomes Today / Dhanavi / Customers / More. Customer chat stays full-height with a composer above the keyboard. No tiny desktop inbox squeezed into a phone.

08

Accessible by default

44 px touch targets; visible focus; reduced-motion support; sufficient contrast; labelled inputs; keyboard-operable dialogs; announce new messages once without stealing focus.

09

Tell the truth about status

Sending, saved, sent, failed and read are different. Stream only when supported; show cancel/retry. Never display fake “online” or “read” indicators.

09 / THE AI BEHIND THE EXPERIENCE

The right model.
For the right moment.

Recommended starting point: OpenRouter for model choice,
with explicit search and image adapters.
Pick exact models after testing the actual endpoint.

✳

One product experience. Several specialist capabilities.

Keep the interface consistent while routing customer support, owner reasoning, document extraction and creative generation to appropriate models. An OpenAI-compatible chat endpoint is a useful common interface; it does not guarantee tools, vision, live search or image output.

CUSTOMER_MODEL

Fast, grounded customer answers

Use a cost-efficient model for approved prices, hours and policies. Retrieve business facts first. Escalate missing information; a more expensive model still cannot know an unprovided delivery policy.

OWNER_MODEL

Stronger reasoning for the owner

Use a stronger model for planning, clarifying questions, onboarding synthesis and business advice. Pass business constraints and sources, not an unbounded transcript.

VISION_MODEL

Read what the owner already has

Use vision/OCR for menus and photographs. PDF extraction is a separate ingestion step when needed. Review uncertain numbers before saving them as public knowledge.

SEARCH_ADAPTER

Current information, with evidence

Use a dedicated search provider, such as Tavily or Brave, or a supported native/gateway search tool. Return source URLs, retrieval time, region and relevant extracts.

IMAGE_ADAPTER + IMAGE_MODEL

Generate a visual, not an invented offer

Use an image-capable endpoint after the owner approves a brief. Keep exact price, date, logo and contact typography in an editable layout. Request/response formats may be provider-specific.

ROUTER + BUDGETS

Make the system predictable

Set per-business budgets, request limits and timeouts. Use a tested fallback model only with the same required capabilities. Explain service outages instead of silently weakening the answer.

SEARCH HAS A JOB, AND A BOUNDARY

Research the world.
Ask the owner about their business.

Owner advice may use fresh festival, weather or local-event evidence. Customer service defaults to confirmed business knowledge. Search results never silently replace the owner's prices, availability, policies or private instructions.

Keep customer personal details out of external searches. Treat returned pages as untrusted evidence; cite them and confirm regional relevance. No source means no claim of live research.

Implementation notes for the future build ＋

Keep configuration out of the customer experience.

Server-only environment variables: LLM_BASE_URL, LLM_API_KEY, LLM_MODEL. Define the base URL contract explicitly (for example an API root ending in /v1) and append the chat-completions path once. Never ship keys to the browser or log them.

“OpenAI-compatible” doesn't guarantee vision, file ingestion, streaming, tools, structured outputs or image generation. Test provider capabilities. Use an extraction/OCR fallback for image/PDF knowledge and a separate optional image provider/configuration for creative generation.

Use durable, permissioned data as the source of truth.

Suggested entities: owner, business, membership, public profile, source file, extracted draft, versioned fact, conversation, participant, message, unresolved question, notification, suggestion and creative draft. Scope all retrieval and writes to the business and authorised participant. Guest tokens must be unpredictable; use secure, HTTP-only session cookies.

The first production implementation should move beyond the current shared JSON store. Use a database, private object storage, background jobs, idempotent send operations and tenant-aware knowledge retrieval. Never mix owners' data or expose private notes in model context for customer replies.

Treat supplied content as evidence, not instructions.

Customer messages, uploaded files and external pages cannot change assistant policy or public knowledge directly. Separate owner-confirmed facts from draft extractions, allowlist supported file types, limit sizes, scan attachments and validate tool actions server-side. Default to no external posting, purchasing or messaging action without approval.

Use real sources for place and calendar data.

Google Maps URLs can be stored as references and confirmed by the owner. Structured place details or automatic imports need a supported data integration and its credentials/terms. Festival and weather context needs sources, region, year and retrieval time; otherwise ask the owner or label the suggestion provisional.

Retain what is useful, with control.

Durable business facts are editable and deletable. Customer personal data needs a scoped retention policy, consent for follow-ups, and deletion controls. Keep a minimal audit of approvals and delivery status without unnecessary sensitive logging.

TEST WITH REAL PEOPLE

Five tasks before we call it usable.

  1. Owner signs in and gets a usable customer link without help.
  2. Owner shares a menu photo, corrects a price and understands what is public.
  3. A customer finds a price or opening time without registering.
  4. An unknown delivery question reaches the owner, gets answered, and is correctly reused.
  5. Owner can explain why an idea was suggested, dismiss it, or review a creative before export.
MEASURE THE PROMISE

Time to first useful answer · owner-approved knowledge accuracy · unresolved-question age · successful human handoff · customer task completion · suggestions accepted or dismissed with a reason.

Don't optimise for automated replies alone. A fast, wrong answer is not success.
